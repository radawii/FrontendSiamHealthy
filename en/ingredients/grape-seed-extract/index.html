Overview

Grape seeds may be a part of the grape that many people overlook, but the seeds are a source of many phytochemicals, particularly proanthocyanidins, compounds in the polyphenol group that are found naturally in many plants.

When grape seeds go through an extraction process, the resulting extract has a polyphenol composition that varies according to the raw material and production method. Oligomeric proanthocyanidins (OPCs) are one of the key compounds that attract particular interest because of their chemical properties and their association with oxidation processes.

Over the past several years, grape seed extract has been studied both in the laboratory and in human trials, particularly in relation to blood vessel markers, oxidative stress and the skin.

Key functions

  • Contains OPCs, which have been studied for their antioxidant properties
  • Has been researched in relation to markers of blood vessel health
  • Has been studied for mechanisms related to oxidative stress and cellular responses
  • Is attracting research interest in relation to the skin and the effects of UV radiation

How it works in the body

Grape seed and how it works in the body

Grape seed extract contains proanthocyanidins, polyphenols with a chemical structure that can take part in reactions with free radicals, which is why they are attracting interest in studies of mechanisms related to oxidative stress.

When consumed, some of the compounds are absorbed and transformed through metabolism, while proanthocyanidins with large structures may be transformed by gut microorganisms before certain metabolites are absorbed.

Researchers are also studying the association of these compounds with the mechanisms of the cells lining the blood vessel walls and the systems that regulate oxidative balance within cells.

Unique properties

Forms and consumption of grape seed

Although grape seeds are naturally found in fresh grapes, the seeds are hard and have an astringent, bitter taste, and the human digestive system cannot break down the structure of fresh seeds to make use of their key compounds. Grape seeds are therefore commonly processed industrially, as follows:

• Grape seed extract: dried grape seeds go through an extraction process and are made into a dry powder, which is filled into dietary supplements in capsule or tablet form. This is the most popular form, as it helps extract the key OPC compounds in amounts the body can use easily.

• Grape seed oil: grape seeds go through cold-press or heat extraction to obtain the oil, which is used both in cooking and as an ingredient in skincare products.

• Grape seed powder: finely ground grape seeds are used as an ingredient in drink mixes or blended into dietary supplements.

Scientific evidence

• Blood vessel health: a 2022 meta-analysis pooling 19 studies found changes in diastolic blood pressure and heart rate, but no significant effect on systolic blood pressure or FMD, with high heterogeneity between studies.

• Blood lipid markers: NCCIH reports that some reviews found interesting effects on LDL and triglycerides, but no firm conclusions can yet be drawn because the studies were relatively small and the extract formulas differed.

• Oxidative stress: there is laboratory-level evidence on the properties of proanthocyanidins, but these results cannot yet be used to directly confirm clinical benefits for cell protection in humans.

• Skin: a 2025 review discussed the potential of grape phytochemicals in the skin's response to UV radiation, but the study results for each compound and form of use still need to be considered separately.

This page is provided for information on composition and nutritional roles and is not advice for treating or preventing any disease.

Safety information

• People taking anticoagulants or antiplatelet medicines should consult a healthcare professional before use, as information on possible interactions is still limited.

This information is educational and is not medical advice. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a medical condition or take medicines, talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using supplements.

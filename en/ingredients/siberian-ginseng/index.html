Overview

Siberian ginseng, with the scientific name Eleutherococcus senticosus, is a herbal plant in the family Araliaceae that has attracted interest in studies on fatigue, physical endurance and the stress response, particularly in the context of the adaptogen concept, which concerns the study of natural substances that may play a role in the body's adaptation processes when facing stress.

The parts commonly used as raw material are the roots and rhizomes, which contain many compounds, especially eleutheroside B and eleutheroside E, which are used as markers in quality testing of certain types of extract. Siberian ginseng differs from Korean ginseng (Panax ginseng) in both its botanical classification and its chemical composition, so data on the effectiveness of the two cannot be used interchangeably.

Key functions

  • Has been scientifically studied in relation to fatigue and weakness
  • Has attracted interest in studies of the body's response to stress
  • Has been researched in relation to endurance and physical performance
  • Has compounds that have been studied in relation to oxidative stress

How it works in the body

Siberian ginseng and its mechanisms in the body

Siberian ginseng is a plant that has attracted interest in studies of the body's response to stress and fatigue, especially the compounds in the eleutheroside group, whose biological activity has been studied at the cellular level and in laboratory animals.

Some research focuses on the relationship of these compounds to the stress-response system, cell function and processes associated with oxidative stress, a condition caused by an imbalance between free radicals and the body's defence systems.

Unique properties

Forms and consumption of Siberian ginseng

Because the fresh root is hard, pungent and leaves a bitter taste on the tongue, it is commonly processed and extracted to draw out its active compounds for use, as follows:

• Herbal tea: the dried root is ground or chopped into small pieces for brewing with hot water.

• Liquid extract: extracted with alcohol or water to draw out the eleutherosides.

• Dietary supplements: in capsule or tablet form, which is the most popular form and allows the most precise control of the amount of active compounds.

Scientific evidence

• Fatigue and weakness: the EMA accepts the use of Siberian ginseng root in traditional herbal medicine for symptoms of fatigue and weakness, based on its long history of use, not on evidence of effectiveness from clinical trials.

• Physical endurance: there have been studies on physical performance and fatigue, but the results are still inconsistent and not sufficient to confirm that it helps increase endurance in the general population.

• Immune system: the biological activity of compounds in Siberian ginseng has been studied, but there is still a lack of clinical evidence of sufficient quality to confirm immune benefits in humans.

This page is provided for information about nutritional roles only. It is not advice for treating or preventing disease.

Safety information

• People with heart disease or abnormal blood pressure should consult a doctor before taking it.

• People taking medications for chronic conditions, especially digoxin, should consult a doctor or pharmacist before use.

• Insomnia, irritability, headache or a rapid heartbeat may occur in some people.

This information is educational and is not medical advice. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a medical condition or take medicines, talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using supplements.

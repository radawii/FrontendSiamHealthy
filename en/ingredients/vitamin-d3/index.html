Overview

Vitamin D3, or cholecalciferol, is a fat-soluble vitamin that the body can produce itself when the skin is exposed to UVB radiation from sunlight. This does not mean, however, that everyone gets enough, especially older adults, people who spend most of their time indoors and people with limited fat absorption.

Vitamin D3 can also be obtained from certain foods, such as salmon, mackerel, egg yolks and vitamin D-fortified foods. Vitamin D3 is converted in the liver and kidneys into a form the body can use to regulate various processes.

Vitamin D3 plays an important role in helping absorb calcium and phosphorus from the intestine. These minerals are essential for building and maintaining strong bones and teeth. Vitamin D3 also contributes to normal muscle function and is involved in regulating the response of immune cells, making it a nutrient that is important to many body systems, not just bone health.

Key functions

  • Helps the absorption and utilisation of calcium and phosphorus
  • Contributes to the maintenance of normal bones and teeth
  • Plays a role in normal muscle function
  • Is involved in the normal function of the immune system
  • Is involved in cell division and cell function
  • Contributes to maintaining balanced blood calcium levels

How it works in the body

Vitamin D3 and its mechanisms in the body

1. The skeletal system and mineral absorption

Vitamin D3 helps regulate the absorption of calcium and phosphorus in the small intestine so that the body has enough minerals for the continuous process of building and remodelling bone. It therefore works together with calcium to maintain normal bones and teeth.

2. The muscular system and movement

Vitamin D is involved in muscle function and in regulating calcium balance, a mineral that is essential for muscle contraction. Having enough vitamin D is therefore one component of maintaining normal muscle function.

3. The immune system and cell function

Many types of immune cells have vitamin D receptors, which are involved in regulating the immune system's responses. Vitamin D therefore plays a role in the normal function of this system.

Unique properties

Sources of vitamin D3

• Sunlight: the skin can produce vitamin D3 when exposed to UVB radiation, but you should not stay in strong sunlight for long periods to increase vitamin D, because UV radiation increases the risk of skin damage.

• Natural foods: such as salmon, mackerel, fish liver oil and egg yolks.

• Vitamin D-fortified foods: such as milk and certain food products with added vitamin D3.

• Dietary supplements: available as capsules, tablets and drops, including formulas combined with calcium or other nutrients.

Scientific evidence

1. Vitamin D3 and maintaining blood vitamin D levels

Studies comparing vitamins D2 and D3 have found that D3 can generally raise blood levels of 25-hydroxyvitamin D, or 25(OH)D, more and maintain those levels for longer across many study designs. This is one of the reasons vitamin D3 is widely used in dietary supplements.

2. Vitamin D3 and bone strength

The VITAL trial in more than 25,000 adults studied supplementation with 2,000 IU of vitamin D3 per day and found that it did not significantly reduce the risk of fractures compared with placebo in adults, most of whom already had sufficient vitamin D levels.

These results help make clear that vitamin D is necessary for bones, but additional supplementation does not mean it will provide extra benefit for everyone.

3. Vitamin D3 and muscle function

Vitamin D is important for muscle function, and vitamin D deficiency may be associated with muscle weakness. However, clinical trials studying vitamin D supplementation for muscle strength and performance have produced inconsistent results, so it should not be claimed that vitamin D3 supplementation increases muscle strength in general.

This information is provided for education about vitamin D3. It is not advice for diagnosing, preventing or treating disease.

Safety information

• Vitamin D3 is a fat-soluble vitamin, so it can accumulate in the body if more is taken than needed.

• Taking high doses of vitamin D3 for a long time may raise blood calcium levels and harm the kidneys or other body systems.

• People with kidney disease, high blood calcium or who are taking certain medications should consult a doctor before taking vitamin D3 supplements.

This information is educational and is not medical advice. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a medical condition or take medicines, talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using supplements.

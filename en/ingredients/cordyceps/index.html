CordycepsTraditional Use, Key Compounds and Modern Research

Cordyceps is a name used for several species of fungi with a history of use in Asian herbal formulas, especially Tibetan cordyceps and golden cordyceps. They contain active compounds of interest such as cordycepin, adenosine and polysaccharides, and are currently being studied in relation to cellular energy production and physical performance.

Overview

Cordyceps is a herb with a story unlike that of ordinary plants, because cordyceps is in fact a fungus, not a root or a herbal plant. Some species of cordyceps grow naturally on insect larvae, while others can be cultivated in controlled environments.

What makes cordyceps interesting is its many groups of natural compounds, especially cordycepin, which is found prominently in golden cordyceps, as well as adenosine and polysaccharides, which have been studied in relation to cell function and energy metabolism. Cordyceps is also currently attracting interest in research on exercise endurance.

Key functions

  • Rich in natural compounds such as cordycepin, adenosine and polysaccharides, with amounts varying by species
  • Has been studied in relation to energy metabolism and cell function
  • Has some clinical studies on physical performance and exercise endurance
  • Contains compounds that have been studied in relation to the immune response at the laboratory level

How it works in the body

Cordyceps and its mechanisms in the body

1. Relationship to cellular energy production

Cordyceps contains certain compounds that have been studied in relation to cellular pathways such as AMPK, which is involved in regulating the body's energy balance, particularly when cells need to adjust energy use and production to suit different conditions.

2. The role of cordycepin in cell function

Cordycepin, or 3'-deoxyadenosine, is a compound with a structure similar to adenosine, a substance involved in many cellular processes. Researchers are studying whether cordycepin may interact with certain signalling pathways associated with the cellular stress response.

3. Immune system response

Polysaccharides from certain fungi have been studied in relation to their interaction with immune cells and changes in signalling molecules associated with the body's responses.

Unique properties

Forms and consumption of cordyceps

Cordyceps is an ancient Chinese herb that today is commonly cultivated in closed systems for safety and freedom from contaminants. It is processed for consumption in the following forms:

• Dried cordyceps: whole cultivated cordyceps fruiting bodies that have been dried can be used to make soup, braised with meat, or brewed as a herbal tea according to traditional Chinese medicine formulas, to enjoy their natural flavour and nutrients.

• Finely ground cordyceps powder: dried cordyceps fruiting bodies are ground into a powder for brewing as a drink or filling into capsules for easy consumption.

• Standardised cordyceps extract: cordyceps undergoes an extraction process to achieve a higher concentration, with the key active compounds such as cordycepin and adenosine controlled at standardised levels. It is commonly filled into dietary supplements in capsule form.

Scientific evidence

1. Studies on exercise endurance

A study by Hirsch et al. (2017) examined a product containing Cordyceps militaris together with other mushrooms in healthy adults, assessing measures of endurance and high-intensity exercise.

Some of the results showed changes in measures of physical performance. However, because the product contained multiple ingredients, it cannot be concluded that all of the effects were due to cordyceps alone, and the number of participants was also limited.

2. Studies on cordyceps use in older adults

A study by Chen et al. (2010) examined a product derived from cultivated Cordyceps sinensis in healthy older adults and found changes in some measures related to exercise performance.

However, the product used in the trial had specific characteristics, and the results do not show that cordyceps can increase strength or physical performance in all population groups.

3. Evidence on cordycepin and cell function

Cordycepin is a compound of interest in laboratory research and has been studied in relation to the regulation of cell function and many biological response processes.

However, most of the evidence is still at the level of cell or animal experiments, so these data are not sufficient to confirm that taking cordyceps extract containing cordycepin will produce the same results in humans.

This information is provided for education about cordyceps. It is not advice for diagnosing, preventing or treating disease.

Safety information

• Each species of cordyceps may have a different chemical composition and safety profile, so check the scientific name and source of the raw material.

• Some users may experience stomach discomfort, nausea, diarrhoea or allergic reactions.

• People taking anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs should consult a doctor before use, as there are concerns about herb-drug interactions.

• People taking immunosuppressants or who have immune system disorders should consult a doctor before use.

• Pregnant women, breastfeeding women and children should avoid using cordyceps extract without advice from a healthcare professional, as safety data are still insufficient.

This information is educational and is not medical advice. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a medical condition or take medicines, talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using supplements.

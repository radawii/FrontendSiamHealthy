Overview

Selenium is a trace mineral, a mineral the body needs only in small amounts. Although the requirement is small, selenium is a component of a specific group of proteins called selenoproteins, which play important roles in cellular processes.

These proteins are involved in regulating oxidative balance in the body, thyroid hormone function and the normal function of the immune system.

The body obtains selenium from foods such as fish, seafood, meat, eggs and grains. The selenium content of plant foods may vary depending on the mineral content of the soil in which they are grown.

Key functions

  • Selenium contributes to the protection of cells from oxidative stress
  • Selenium contributes to the protection of cells from free radicals
  • Selenium contributes to the maintenance of normal hair
  • Selenium contributes to the maintenance of normal nails
  • Selenium contributes to the normal function of the immune system
  • Selenium contributes to normal thyroid function

How it works in the body

What does selenium do?

• A shield for cells: is a component of enzymes that help maintain the balance of oxidative reactions within cells

• Supports the thyroid: works with enzymes that help convert thyroid hormones into their active form

• Supports immunity: contributes to the natural function and responses of immune cells

• A component of key proteins: is incorporated into selenoproteins that drive various mechanisms in the body

Selenium and its mechanisms in the body

Selenium that enters the body is used as a key component in building specialised proteins called selenoproteins, a major group of proteins that regulate biochemical reactions and keep cellular-level functions working normally.

One of the key mechanisms of this group of proteins is serving as a main component of enzymes in the antioxidant system, such as glutathione peroxidase, which helps deal with free radicals that form naturally during energy metabolism, as well as working with enzymes in thyroid tissue to maintain the balance of thyroid hormone function.

The production and combined action of these proteins is a fundamental mechanism that helps the various systems within cells maintain balance and function normally from day to day.

Unique properties

Food sources of selenium

• Seafood and marine fish: short mackerel, tuna (both fresh and canned), sardines, shrimp, crab, cockles and oysters

• Meat and offal: pork, beef, chicken, as well as pork liver and chicken liver

• Eggs and dairy products: chicken eggs, duck eggs, cow's milk and yoghurt

• Grains and rice: brown rice, Riceberry rice, Hom Mali (jasmine) rice and oats

• Nuts and seeds: sunflower seeds, cashews, peanuts, white and black sesame seeds

• Mushrooms and local vegetables: shiitake mushrooms, straw mushrooms, Indian oyster mushrooms, garlic and asparagus

Scientific evidence

• The role of selenoproteins: selenium is a component of proteins involved in regulating oxidative balance, thyroid hormone function and other biological processes.

• The immune system: selenium plays a role in the function of immune cells, but the evidence does not confirm that selenium supplementation in people who already get enough will always improve immunity.

• Selenium from yeast: is a source of selenium used in nutritional studies and dietary supplements. However, research outcomes depend on the form of the compound, the dose and the population studied.

This page is provided for information about selenium's nutritional role only and is not advice for treating or preventing any disease.

Safety information

• Excessive selenium intake, especially from taking several dietary supplements together, may cause nausea, digestive disturbances, hair loss or brittle nails.

• Continuous intake of high amounts of selenium may be harmful to health, so the recommended amount should not be exceeded without advice from a specialist.

• People with underlying health conditions, who take regular medication or are considering taking several dietary supplements together should consult a doctor or pharmacist before use.

• People who are allergic to yeast should check the source and allergen information of products that use selenium-enriched yeast before consumption.

This information is educational and is not medical advice. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a medical condition or take medicines, talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using supplements.

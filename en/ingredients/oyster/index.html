Overview

Oysters are not just a popular seafood; they are also a source of many important nutrients, all of which play a role in the body's processes, from building and repairing cells to immune function and overall health.

One of the nutrients that draws particular attention to oysters is zinc, an essential mineral that the body cannot produce on its own. It is involved in the activity of a large number of enzymes, protein synthesis, cell division and the normal function of the immune system. Zinc also plays a role in reproductive processes and normal hormone function, making it one of the nutrients of importance to men's health.

Oysters also contain vitamin B12 and iron, which are involved in red blood cell formation and the transport of oxygen to tissues, while selenium is a component of certain enzymes involved in the body's defence against oxidative stress. Protein and amino acids are important for building and repairing tissue and for maintaining normal muscle function.

Key functions

  • A natural food source of zinc, which is involved in the normal function of the immune system
  • Contains zinc, which is essential for protein synthesis and cell division
  • Contains minerals involved in the body's normal reproductive processes
  • Contains vitamin B12, which is essential for red blood cell formation and nervous system function
  • Contains protein and several minerals that form part of a balanced diet

How it works in the body

Oysters and their mechanisms in the body

1. Cellular and immune function

Oysters are a seafood containing protein, amino acids and several important minerals that the body uses in the formation and function of immune cells, particularly zinc and selenium, which play a role in cellular function and in the defence against oxidative stress. Adequate intake of these nutrients therefore plays an important part in maintaining normal immune function.

2. The reproductive system and male hormones

Oysters have attracted interest in men's nutrition because they contain nutrients involved in reproductive function, especially zinc, which plays a role in sperm production and the maintenance of normal testosterone levels.

3. Muscles and tissue repair

Once protein from oysters has been digested and absorbed, the body can use it to build muscle protein, repair tissue and support cellular function.

Unique properties

Forms and consumption of oysters

Oysters can be consumed and processed in many forms, both as ordinary food and as extracts used as ingredients in dietary supplements. Each form differs in its characteristics and nutritional composition, as follows:

• Fresh or cooked oyster meat: commonly eaten as food; thorough cooking helps reduce the risk from pathogens

• Dried oyster meat: dehydrated to extend shelf life and used as a food ingredient

• Oyster powder: made from oyster meat that has been dried and ground into a powder

• Oyster extract: produced through an extraction process to obtain certain groups of compounds from oyster meat

• Dietary supplements: use oyster extract as an ingredient, possibly combined with other vitamins or minerals

Scientific evidence

1. Nutritional value and the immune system

A review by Negara et al. (2022), which compiled 37 studies on the Pacific oyster (Crassostrea gigas), found that oyster meat contains protein, essential amino acids, glycogen, fatty acids and a wide range of minerals. In addition, compounds isolated from oysters showed antioxidant and immunomodulatory properties in some studies.

However, most of the evidence comes from laboratory experiments, so it cannot be concluded that eating oysters directly boosts immunity in humans.

2. Studies on the male reproductive system

Li et al. (2020) studied oyster peptides and oyster powder in male mice with reproductive damage induced by cyclophosphamide, tracking hormonal markers and the condition of testicular tissue. The study found that the group given oyster peptides showed favourable changes in some markers, including androgen levels and oxidative stress in the tissue.

However, this was an animal study, so effects on hormones, fertility or sexual performance in humans cannot yet be confirmed.

3. Studies on muscle strength

Lee (2022) conducted a trial in 54 adults aged 50–78 years in which the test group took 1,200 mg of fermented oyster extract per day for 12 weeks together with walking exercise, compared with a placebo group.

The study found that muscle strength and muscle mass did not increase significantly compared with the control group. Although the extract contains interesting amino acids and compounds, the results therefore do not yet support claims that oyster extract directly increases muscle mass or strength.

This information is provided to educate about oysters and is not advice for diagnosing, preventing or treating any disease.

Safety information

• People who are allergic to shellfish or molluscan seafood should avoid products containing oyster ingredients.

• Raw or undercooked oysters may contain Vibrio and other pathogens, so they should be cooked thoroughly before eating, especially by people with liver disease or weakened immunity.

• Excessive intake of zinc from dietary supplements over a long period may interfere with copper absorption and cause side effects.

• Pregnant and breastfeeding women should consult a healthcare professional before using concentrated extracts, as product-specific safety data may be limited.

This information is educational and is not medical advice. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a medical condition or take medicines, talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using supplements.

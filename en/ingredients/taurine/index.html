Overview

Taurine is a compound in the amino sulfonic acid group that is found naturally in many tissues of the body. Although it is often classed as an amino acid, taurine has a chemical structure different from the amino acids used to build proteins and is not directly incorporated into protein chains.

What makes taurine of interest is its diverse roles at the cellular level, from regulating the balance of water and solutes inside cells and supporting the function of nerve tissue, to binding with bile acids to form compounds that are important for the digestion and absorption of fat.

Although taurine is a common ingredient in energy drinks, it does not stimulate the central nervous system in the same way as caffeine.

Key functions

  • Plays a role in maintaining the balance of water and solutes inside cells
  • Is involved in the function of the heart and heart muscle
  • Plays a role in the function and signalling of the nervous system
  • Is a component in the formation of bile acid compounds involved in the digestion and absorption of fat
  • Is involved in maintaining calcium balance and the function of muscle cells
  • Has a physiological role in retinal tissue

How it works in the body

Taurine and how it works in the body

1. Maintaining balance within cells

Taurine acts as one of the osmoregulators, which are involved in adjusting the balance of water and solutes inside cells, helping cells adapt to changes in their environment through physiological mechanisms.

2. Heart and nervous system function

Taurine is found in heart and nervous system tissue in biologically significant amounts and is involved in regulating intracellular calcium, cell membrane function and certain nerve signalling processes.

3. Formation of bile compounds

Taurine can bind with bile acids produced by the liver to form compounds called taurine-conjugated bile acids, which help bile acids perform their role in the digestion and absorption of fat properly.

Unique properties

Food sources of taurine

• Fish and seafood, such as tuna, salmon, mussels and oysters

• Meat, such as beef, pork and lamb

• Poultry, such as chicken and turkey

• Milk and dairy products, which contain varying amounts of taurine depending on the type of product

Scientific evidence

1. Studies on cardiovascular health

A systematic review and meta-analysis published in Nutrition Journal in 2024 evaluated clinical studies on taurine and cardiovascular markers, and found positive signals in some markers, such as blood pressure.

2. Evidence on energy and physical performance

Although taurine is a common ingredient in energy drinks, evidence on improving physical performance and brain function remains inconsistent.

3. Research on taurine and age-related changes

Taurine has attracted research interest in animal studies on the ageing process. However, a study published in the journal Science in 2025, which examined data from humans and several groups of animals, found that blood taurine levels did not consistently decline with age and are not yet suitable for use as a general marker of ageing.

Safety information

• Taurine obtained from ordinary food is considered part of a normal diet. When used in supplement form, the amount and the product's safety information should be considered.

• People with underlying medical conditions, especially heart disease, blood pressure disorders or kidney disease, should consult a doctor before using dietary supplements containing taurine.

• Pregnant women, breastfeeding women and children should not use high doses of taurine in supplement form without advice from a healthcare professional.

• Energy drinks should not be used as a main source of taurine, as they may contain caffeine, sugar and other ingredients that also need to be considered.

• There is not yet an official recommended daily intake of taurine for the general population, so supplement doses should not be self-determined based on clinical trial results alone.

This information is educational and is not medical advice. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a medical condition or take medicines, talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using supplements.

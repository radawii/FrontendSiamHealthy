AstragalusAn Ancient Herbal Root Through the Lens of Modern Science

Astragalus (known in Thai as Pak Kee or Aung Kee) is a herbal plant of the legume family whose root has a long history of use in traditional Chinese medicine. The root contains several groups of natural compounds, including polysaccharides, astragalosides and flavonoids, which have been studied for their relationship with cellular function, the immune system and antioxidant processes.

Overview

Astragalus (Astragalus membranaceus), known in Chinese as Huang Qi, is a herbal plant native to East Asia. The part most commonly used is the root, which is usually dried and cut into thin slices. In the past, astragalus was an ingredient in soups and Chinese herbal formulas, before being developed into powder and extract raw materials for today's dietary supplements.

What makes astragalus root interesting is the diversity of its natural constituents: more than 100 compounds have been identified, covering polysaccharides, saponins, flavonoids and amino acids, in particular Astragalus Polysaccharides (APS) and Astragaloside IV, key compounds whose biological properties have been studied in many areas.

Building on traditional herbal knowledge, modern science has extended the study of astragalus to cellular-level mechanisms, including communication between immune cells, oxidative processes and blood vessel function.

Key functions

  • Contains Astragalus Polysaccharides (APS), which have been studied for their effects on the immune response
  • Contains Astragaloside IV, which has been studied for its relationship with cellular and blood vessel function
  • Contains flavonoids, which have been studied for their antioxidant properties

How it works in the body

Astragalus and its mechanisms in the body

1. The immune system and communication between cells

The immune system relies on communication between many types of cells to regulate the body's responses. The polysaccharides in astragalus have been studied for their interactions with immune cells such as macrophages and lymphocytes, as well as the production of cytokines, proteins that act as signals between cells.

2. Oxidative balance within cells

During normal metabolism, the body continuously produces free radicals. The flavonoids and astragalosides in astragalus have therefore been studied in biological models to understand the interactions between these plant compounds and the antioxidant mechanisms within cells.

3. Function of the cells lining the blood vessels

The cells lining the blood vessels play a role in regulating the constriction and relaxation of blood vessels through several signalling molecules, one of which is nitric oxide. Laboratory studies of Astragaloside IV have covered these signalling mechanisms, which form the basis for studying astragalus in the context of heart and circulatory health.

Unique properties

Forms and consumption of astragalus

Dried astragalus root is commonly used as an ingredient in soups, herbal teas and Chinese herbal formulas.

Astragalus powder is made from root that has been dried and finely ground, retaining several groups of constituents of the original raw material.

Astragalus extract is produced through an extraction process that isolates certain groups of compounds from the root.

Standardised astragalus extract is an extract with a specified content of certain key compounds, such as polysaccharides or Astragaloside IV.

Scientific evidence

1. Astragalus and the immune response

A 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 19 human studies with a total of 1,094 participants to evaluate the relationship between astragalus use and immune markers. It found changes in some markers, such as cytokine levels and the proportions of immune cells.

However, most of the studies analysed were small and differed in their study methods, so the findings are not yet sufficient to confirm that taking astragalus can boost immunity or prevent illness in the general population.

2. Astragalus polysaccharides and their biological properties

A review article published in the journal Chinese Herbal Medicines in 2025 compiled data on the structure, extraction methods and biological properties of polysaccharides from astragalus, explaining that differences in molecular structure and extraction methods are key factors that may affect the properties of the resulting compounds.

This review helps explain that, even though the polysaccharides come from the same plant, their biological properties may differ depending on the production process, and further human studies are still needed.

3. Astragalosides, flavonoids and cellular-level function

A 2023 review examined data on flavonoids, saponins and polysaccharides from astragalus, covering both how these compounds are produced within the plant and the biological properties found in experiments, particularly mechanisms related to oxidative balance, the immune response and the cardiovascular system.

This information provides a basis for developing future clinical research, but it cannot yet be used to confirm the effectiveness of taking astragalus in daily life.

*This information is provided to educate about astragalus and is not advice for diagnosing, preventing or treating any disease.

Safety information

People with autoimmune diseases should avoid taking astragalus, as it may affect immune system function.

Astragalus may interact with immunosuppressant drugs. People who take regular medication should consult a doctor or pharmacist before using products containing astragalus.

Pregnant and breastfeeding women should avoid dietary supplements containing astragalus, as safety data are still insufficient.

This information is educational and is not medical advice. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a medical condition or take medicines, talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using supplements.

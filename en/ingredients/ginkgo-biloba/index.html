Overview

Ginkgo biloba leaf is one of the herbal plants attracting a great deal of interest in studies on memory, brain function and blood circulation, particularly among older adults and people interested in looking after their brain health as they age. Although ginkgo leaf has a long history of use, what makes this plant of interest today is the natural compounds found in its leaves, which scientists study to understand how they work in the body.

The key compounds in ginkgo leaf are flavonoids and terpene lactones, which have been studied for their antioxidant properties as well as mechanisms related to nerve cells and blood vessels. These compounds are therefore a key reason why ginkgo leaf continues to be studied today in relation to brain health and blood circulation.

What are the key compounds in ginkgo leaf?

1. Flavonoids

A group of plant compounds that have shown antioxidant properties in laboratory experiments. Ginkgo leaf extract commonly contains derivatives of quercetin, kaempferol and isorhamnetin.

2. Terpene lactones

These include ginkgolides and bilobalide, compounds found specifically in ginkgo that have been studied in relation to mechanisms involving the nervous system and platelet function. However, the results of these mechanistic studies cannot yet be used to directly confirm clinical benefits.

Key functions

  • Contains flavonoid and terpene lactone compounds
  • Some of its compounds have been studied for antioxidant activity at the laboratory level
  • Is attracting interest in studies on mechanisms related to the nervous system and blood circulation
  • A plant extract with clinical research on memory and brain function

How it works in the body

Ginkgo leaf and how it works in the body

1. Role in blood circulation

Some compounds in ginkgo leaf have been studied in relation to blood vessel function and blood circulation, particularly ginkgolides, which affect signalling molecules involved in platelet aggregation.

2. Protecting cells from oxidative stress

Ginkgo leaf contains flavonoids, which have shown antioxidant properties in laboratory studies and are associated with reducing the reactions of free radicals that may affect cell components.

3. Association with the function of the nervous system and brain

The key compounds in ginkgo leaf have been studied in relation to nerve cell function, including signalling between cells and the response of cells to stress.

Unique properties

Forms and consumption of ginkgo leaf

Because fresh ginkgo leaves naturally contain ginkgolic acid, which must be removed and strictly controlled according to safety standards, they are not usually eaten fresh but are processed through extraction instead. The main forms are as follows:

• Standardised extract in dietary supplements: in capsule form, the most popular form and the one supported by the most research. It goes through an extraction process to standardise the proportions of key compounds to appropriate levels with high purity.

• Ginkgo leaf herbal tea: ginkgo leaves that have been dried and processed for safety are ground for brewing as a drink.

• Liquid extract: key compounds are extracted in concentrated liquid form for use as an ingredient in health formulas.

Scientific evidence

• Memory and brain function: ginkgo leaf extract has been studied in relation to memory and thinking ability, but there is no clear evidence that it helps improve memory or concentration in healthy people.

• Brain health in older adults: a large study in more than 3,000 older adults did not find that ginkgo leaf extract helped reduce the risk of dementia or slow the decline in thinking ability.

• Blood circulation: some compounds in ginkgo leaf have been studied in relation to mechanisms involving blood vessels and platelets, but there is not yet enough evidence to confirm benefits for blood circulation in general.

• Antioxidants: the flavonoids in ginkgo leaf have shown antioxidant activity in laboratory studies, but clinical benefits in humans cannot yet be directly concluded.

This page is provided for information on nutritional roles only and is not advice for treating or preventing any disease.

Safety information

• Ginkgo leaf extract may cause adverse effects such as headache, dizziness or digestive disturbances, and may increase the risk of bleeding when used together with anticoagulants or medicines that affect platelets.

• People with bleeding disorders, people who are about to undergo surgery, or people who take medication regularly should consult a doctor before using products containing ginkgo leaf extract.

• Use by pregnant women without medical advice is not recommended, and safety data are not yet sufficient for breastfeeding women.

This information is educational and is not medical advice. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a medical condition or take medicines, talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using supplements.

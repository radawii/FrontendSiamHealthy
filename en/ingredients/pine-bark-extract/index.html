Overview

Pine bark extract is a natural extract obtained from the bark of certain pine species. Pine bark contains several important compounds, especially polyphenols such as procyanidins, catechin and epicatechin, which have notable antioxidant properties.

Pine bark is also associated with vascular processes, particularly the function of the vascular endothelium and nitric oxide, which plays a part in regulating the dilation of blood vessels. Certain compounds in pine bark are also associated with the body's natural inflammatory response processes.

Another area of interest is skin health: certain pine bark extract formulas have also been studied for their relationship with skin hydration, elasticity and processes related to collagen and hyaluronic acid.

Key functions

  • A source of polyphenol compounds, especially procyanidins
  • Its antioxidant properties have been studied at the laboratory level
  • There is research on oxidative stress and cellular responses
  • Has attracted interest in studies of vascular function
  • Has been studied for its relationship with skin structure and condition in humans

How it works in the body

Pine bark and its mechanisms in the body

1. Protecting cells from oxidative stress

Pine bark extract contains polyphenols that have been studied for their ability to react with free radicals and their relationship with the systems that regulate oxidative balance within cells. Laboratory experiments have found that certain compounds may affect these processes.

2. The vascular system and endothelial function

Certain pine bark extract formulas have been studied in relation to the function of the endothelium, the layer of cells lining the inside of blood vessels, particularly processes involving nitric oxide, which plays a role in regulating the dilation of blood vessels.

3. The skin and tissue structure

Pine bark extract has attracted interest in studies of processes related to collagen and hyaluronic acid, key components of skin structure. Some studies have found changes in skin hydration, elasticity and dark spots after the extract was taken.

Unique properties

Forms and consumption of pine bark extract

• Dried pine bark: raw material from the bark of pine trees that has been dried for use in processing or extraction

• Powdered pine bark extract: extract that has been dried and ground into a powder for use as an ingredient in products

• Standardised extract: extract with a specified content of certain groups of key compounds according to the manufacturer's specifications

• Combination dietary supplements: pine bark extract used together with vitamins, minerals or other plant extracts

Scientific evidence

1. Studies on skin hydration and elasticity

A study in 112 women with signs of photoaged skin examined oral intake of French maritime pine bark extract for 12 weeks and found that skin condition scores and pigmentation levels of dark spots decreased significantly on some measures.

However, the study did not have a placebo group for direct comparison, so it cannot yet be confirmed that the observed effects were due to the extract alone.

2. Studies on vascular function

A systematic review published in 2025, pooling 27 randomised trials with a total of 1,685 participants, found that the use of pine bark extract was associated with changes in some markers, such as blood pressure, blood sugar levels and low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol, but found no significant differences in other markers, such as high-density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol and triglycerides.

The findings therefore reflect a scientific possibility rather than confirmation of the effectiveness of every extract formula.

3. Studies on skin pigmentation processes

A randomised, placebo-controlled, double-blind trial in 44 women studied the use of the pine bark extract Pycnogenol® together with standard skincare products for 60 days and found that the group receiving the extract showed greater changes in skin pigmentation scores than the placebo group.

However, this result comes from a study of a specific extract formula used together with other skincare methods, so conclusions cannot be drawn for pine bark extracts in general.

This information is provided to educate about pine bark and is not advice for diagnosing, preventing or treating any disease.

Safety information

• Taking pine bark extract may cause stomach discomfort, nausea, dizziness or headache in some people.

• People taking anticoagulant or antiplatelet medicines should consult a doctor or pharmacist before use.

• People taking blood-sugar-lowering or blood-pressure-lowering medicines should consult a healthcare professional before taking concentrated extracts.

• Pregnant and breastfeeding women should avoid using concentrated extracts without advice from a specialist.

This information is educational and is not medical advice. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a medical condition or take medicines, talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using supplements.

Overview

Fish oil is oil obtained from the tissues of fish, especially oily fish such as salmon, sardines and mackerel. Its key feature is that it is a source of omega-3 fatty acids, particularly eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), fatty acids involved in the structure of cell membranes and the normal functioning of the body.

Although the terms fish oil and omega-3 are often mentioned together, they do not mean exactly the same thing. Omega-3 is a group of fatty acids found in many food sources, while fish oil is one source of EPA and DHA. DHA is found as an important component of brain cells and the retina, while EPA is involved in the production of signalling molecules in the body, particularly processes related to the inflammatory response.

What is the difference between fish oil and omega-3?

Many people may think that fish oil and omega-3 are the same thing, but in fact fish oil is a source of omega-3 fatty acids, while omega-3 is a group of fatty acids found in both fish-based and plant-based foods, with different sources and different roles in the body, as follows:

Omega-3 fatty acidSources and properties
EPAFound in fish and fish oil; involved in the production of signalling molecules in the body
DHAFound in fish and fish oil; an important component of the brain and retina
ALAFound in flaxseed, chia seeds and some vegetable oils; the body can convert it into EPA and DHA in limited amounts

Key functions

  • A source of omega-3 fatty acids, particularly EPA and DHA
  • EPA and DHA contribute to the normal function of the heart
  • DHA is an important component of the cell membranes of the brain and retina
  • Omega-3 fatty acids are involved in the structure and function of cell membranes
  • EPA and DHA are precursors of certain signalling molecules involved in inflammatory processes

How it works in the body

Fish oil and how it works in the body

1. The cardiovascular system

Fish oil is a source of EPA and DHA, which play a role in the functioning of the cardiovascular system. Both fatty acids can become components of cell membranes and are involved in the production of signalling molecules in the body. The effects of EPA and DHA on blood triglyceride levels have also been studied.

2. The nervous system and vision

DHA is a fatty acid found in high amounts in the membranes of nerve cells and the retina, especially in the photoreceptor cells, and is important for signalling and the normal functioning of the visual system.

3. Response to inflammatory processes

EPA and DHA from fish oil can be used as precursors for producing compounds involved in the body's natural processes for regulating and resolving inflammation, such as resolvins and protectins. The role of these fatty acids has therefore been studied in the context of cell function and the inflammatory response.

Unique properties

Forms and consumption of fish oil

• Liquid fish oil: oil extracted from fish and processed according to specified standards.

• Fish oil capsules: fish oil filled into capsules for convenient consumption.

• Fish oil powder: fish oil processed into a powder, often using technology that encapsulates the oil particles.

• Foods containing omega-3: such as salmon, sardines and mackerel, which are natural sources of EPA and DHA.

Scientific evidence

1. EPA and DHA and the cardiovascular system

Information from the National Institutes of Health (NIH) explains that EPA and DHA play an important role in the functioning of the cardiovascular system, and there is clinical research on their effects on triglyceride levels.

However, evidence on reducing the risk of cardiovascular disease with omega-3 supplements varies according to the formulation, dose and population studied.

2. DHA and the brain and eyes

DHA is an important component of the cell membranes of the brain and retina and plays a role in the structure and function of these cells. Clinical research is still studying the relationship between DHA intake and cognitive function and eye health.

However, it cannot yet be concluded that fish oil supplementation helps improve brain function or prevent vision degeneration in the general population.

3. EPA and DHA and inflammatory processes

Biochemical studies have found that EPA and DHA are precursors of signalling molecules involved in the body's natural regulation and resolution of inflammation. Although these mechanisms continue to be studied, clinical trial results vary according to health status and the form of fatty acid intake.

Safety information

• Taking fish oil may cause fishy burps, nausea, stomach discomfort or diarrhoea in some people.

• People who are allergic to seafish or fish oil should avoid products containing these ingredients.

• People taking anticoagulants or antiplatelet medicines should consult a doctor before using fish oil products, especially in high doses.

• People with blood clotting disorders or who are scheduled for surgery should inform their doctor about the products they are taking.

• High intakes of omega-3 may increase the risk of atrial fibrillation in some populations.

This information is educational and is not medical advice. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a medical condition or take medicines, talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using supplements.

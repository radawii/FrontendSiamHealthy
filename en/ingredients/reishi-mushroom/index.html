Overview

In ancient Chinese culture, the reishi mushroom, or lingzhi, was one of the mushrooms symbolising good fortune, longevity and the wish for good health, earning it the nickname “the mushroom of immortality”, which reflects the cultural values and beliefs of the past.

Today, reishi mushroom is attracting interest in studies of its health benefits in several areas, particularly the immune response, fatigue and quality of life. The key compounds researchers are interested in include polysaccharides, particularly beta-glucans, and triterpenes, which have been studied for their biological properties and their association with cellular processes.

Key functions

  • Its key compounds have been studied for their role in the immune response
  • Is attracting interest in studies on fatigue and quality of life

How it works in the body

Reishi mushroom and how it works in the body

1. The immune response

Polysaccharides, particularly beta-glucans from reishi mushroom, have been studied for their relationship with the function of immune cells such as macrophages and certain white blood cells. Experimental results have found that these compounds may affect signalling between cells and immune response processes.

2. Processes that protect cells from oxidative stress

Reishi mushroom contains compounds that have been studied for their antioxidant properties in the laboratory, particularly triterpenes and certain phenolic compounds, which may be associated with the cellular response to oxidative stress.

3. Regulation of inflammatory processes

Cell and animal studies have found that some compounds from reishi mushroom may affect the activity of signalling molecules involved in inflammation, such as cytokines, which are part of the body's natural response processes.

4. Fatigue and quality of life

There have been some clinical studies on the use of reishi mushroom extract in people experiencing fatigue, which assessed various markers and reported results in a favourable direction.

Unique properties

Forms and consumption of reishi mushroom

The natural reishi fruiting body is hard and woody with a bitter taste, so it is not cooked fresh like ordinary mushrooms but is processed to be consumed in various forms, as follows:

• Dried sliced reishi: fresh fruiting bodies are dried and cut into slices to be boiled in hot water and drunk as an herbal infusion.

• Reishi powder: dried fruiting bodies are ground into a fine powder, which is filled into capsules or dissolved in water to drink.

• Reishi extract: reishi goes through a decoction-extraction process with water or alcohol and is then made into a dry powder to be filled into dietary supplements in capsule or tablet form.

• Reishi spores: the spores (the brown powder released by the mushroom) go through a process that cracks their outer shell so the body can use them more easily.

Scientific evidence

• Polysaccharides and triterpenes from reishi mushroom have been studied at the cellular level and in laboratory animals, but these results cannot yet be used to directly confirm effectiveness in humans.

• Research on the immune system has produced varied results, and there is not yet enough clinical evidence to confirm the benefits of reishi mushroom supplements in the general population.

• Some studies on fatigue and quality of life have had limited numbers of participants or used extracts with different compositions.

This page is provided for information on nutritional roles only and is not advice for treating or preventing any disease.

Safety information

• Nausea, stomach discomfort, dry mouth, dizziness or an itchy rash may occur in some people.

• People taking anticoagulants or antiplatelet medicines should consult a doctor before use.

• People taking immunosuppressants should consult a doctor before use.

• People taking blood-pressure-lowering medicines or medicines to control blood sugar levels should consult a doctor or pharmacist before use.

• People with liver disease should be cautious, as there have been reports of liver injury possibly associated with reishi mushroom products, although this is rare.

This information is educational and is not medical advice. If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, have a medical condition or take medicines, talk to a doctor or pharmacist before using supplements.
